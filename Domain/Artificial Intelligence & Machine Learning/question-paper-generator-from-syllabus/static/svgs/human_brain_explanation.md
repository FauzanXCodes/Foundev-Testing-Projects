3. Common student misconceptions:
  - The belief that the brain is composed of only one part (e.g., the "thinking part") when it consists of various regions, each with a unique function.
  - The idea that the brain is a static organ, unchanging throughout an individual's life, rather than being dynamic and adaptable.
  - Misunderstanding the role of different brain structures in cognitive processes (e.g., memory formation, emotion regulation, problem-solving).

4. Interactive learning suggestions:
  - Labeling exercise: Students can practice identifying various parts of the human brain by labeling them on the diagram or a similar version with more details.
  - Quiz or game: Create an interactive quiz or game where students have to answer questions related to the structure and functions of different brain regions.
  - Virtual dissection: Offer virtual dissections or 3D models of the human brain, allowing students to explore its intricate structure in detail.
  - Brain-based activities: Encourage students to participate in brain-based activities, such as memory games and problem-solving exercises, to better understand how their brains work in practice.
