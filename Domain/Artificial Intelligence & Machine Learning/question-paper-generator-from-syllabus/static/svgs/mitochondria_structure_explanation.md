2. Real-world applications:
   - Understanding the structure of mitochondria is crucial in various fields such as biology, medicine, and bioenergetics. It helps in understanding cellular respiration, oxidative phosphorylation, and apoptosis (cell death). This knowledge can be applied in developing treatments for diseases like Parkinson's and Alzheimer's that have links to mitochondrial dysfunction.

3. Common student misconceptions:
   - Many students might struggle with understanding the intricate structure of mitochondria, especially the differences between the outer membrane, inner membrane, cristae, and matrix. Misconceptions may also arise when students confuse the role of mitochondria in cellular respiration with other cellular processes.
   - Another common misconception is that only eukaryotic cells contain mitochondria; however, some prokaryotes (e.g., mitochondrion-like organelles) do have similar organelles.
