2. Real-world applications:
   - The refraction of light through a prism has numerous real-world applications such as in dispersion spectroscopy (where spectra can be analyzed to identify components), polarizing filters (used in camera lenses, safety glasses, and LCD screens), and even the design of optical fibers for communication.

3. Common student misconceptions:
   - One common misconception is that light always bends towards the normal (perpendicular to the surface) when it passes through a medium with a lower refractive index, which is actually true only when the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle.
   - Another misunderstanding can be about the amount of dispersion, where students may think that all colors are dispersed equally or assume incorrect order of colors in a spectrum (ROYGBIV versus actual order: violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, and red).

4. Interactive learning suggestions:
   - Virtual labs or simulations allowing users to manipulate the angle of incidence, prism angles, and types of materials for refraction could help students visualize and understand this concept better.
   - Augmented reality apps that enable users to observe real-world light refraction through different objects such as prisms, glasses, or liquids can facilitate hands-on learning.
   - Interactive quizzes with questions targeting common misconceptions and reinforcing the proper understanding of refraction could also be beneficial.
   For a more engaging experience, consider incorporating gamification elements like points, leaderboards, and rewards to motivate students during their learning journey.
