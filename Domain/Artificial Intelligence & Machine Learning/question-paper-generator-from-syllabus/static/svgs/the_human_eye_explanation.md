2. Real-world applications:
   - Understanding the structure and function of the human eye is crucial in various fields such as medicine, optometry, psychology, computer graphics, photography, and even animation. It helps individuals to have better awareness about eye health and vision issues, allowing them to take care of their eyes and seek professional help when necessary.
   - In computer graphics and photography, knowing how the human eye perceives light, color, and depth can lead to more realistic digital renderings and captures. This knowledge also plays a significant role in designing user interfaces for virtual reality applications, ensuring they are comfortable and intuitive for users.

3. Common student misconceptions:
   - Some students may struggle with understanding how the cornea, pupil, iris, and lens work together to focus light onto the retina. A common mistake is assuming that the iris regulates the amount of light entering the eye, when it actually controls the size of the pupil for this purpose.
   - Another misconception relates to depth perception; some students may believe that both eyes see exactly the same image and that depth is added through software or filters after the fact. In reality, the slight differences between the two images enable our brain to create a sense of depth.

4. Interactive learning suggestions:
   - Interactive 3D models or animations that allow students to explore the structure of the eye from different angles can help them visualize and understand its complexities better. These tools can be supplemented with explanatory text, quizzes, and activities designed to reinforce learning.
   - Virtual Reality (VR) simulations can offer immersive experiences that demonstrate depth perception in action. For instance, students could use VR goggles to explore a 3D environment where objects appear at different depths based on their position relative to the viewer's eyes. This hands-on approach encourages active learning and helps students better retain information about the human eye.
   - Gamification can also be effective, with educational games that challenge students to manipulate an interactive model of the eye or correct common misconceptions about its function. Such games can make learning engaging, competitive, and fun while still reinforcing key concepts related to the human eye.
